Market types

How a market works

Ask. Trade. Resolve. Redeem.

  1. 01

    Ask

    Anyone opens a market: a question, a market type and an end date.

  2. 02

    Trade

    Each outcome is a token. Its price reads as the crowd’s probability.

  3. 03

    Resolve

    After the end date Reality.eth reports the answer. A dispute goes to Kleros.

  4. 04

    Redeem

    Holders of the winning outcome redeem their tokens for the collateral.

  • Categorical

    Choose one of several outcomes.

  • Scalar

    Forecast a number within a range.

  • Multi-categorical

    Several outcomes can be true at once.

  • Multi-scalar

    Several numbers forecast side by side.

  • Conditional, any type

    Any market can depend on an outcome of another market.

01Seer app

The explorer for every market on Seer.

Think of it as Seer’s Etherscan. Whatever app a market was made in, it shows up here: categorical, scalar, multi-categorical and multi-scalar markets, and conditional markets built on them, with its prices, liquidity and resolution status in one place.

Open the Seer app

02Futarchy

See the outcome of a vote before you cast it.

For each proposal, two conditional markets price the DAO’s chosen success metric: one if the proposal passes, one if it fails. The gap between them is a signal. It informs the decision, the DAO still makes it.

Open Futarchy

03Deep

Questions with many possible answers.

Deep handles fields of outcomes too wide for a yes/no market, including markets built for AI agents to trade. For example, agents forecast how human evaluators will score each proposal in a grant round, so reviewers know where to look first.

Open Deep

04Opportunity Markets

Forecast the pick. Reviewers still make it.

Traders forecast which submissions will be selected, for a grant, a hire, a call for proposals. The market surfaces strong entries early. The final decision stays with the reviewers.

Open Opportunity Markets

05Risk Pricing

Compare risk across protocols, not one yes/no.

A single “will it be exploited?” market gives you one price. Risk Pricing lines up market-estimated risk across many DeFi protocols on the same question and horizon, so they can be compared side by side.

Open Risk Pricing
